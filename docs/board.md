# Using the board

## The ticket list

The ticket list beside the card, on the Mac board and the phone, has four sections: Doing for what is active, Docked for the current sprint, Deferred for a later sprint, and Done. Doing and Docked show first; the small triangle beside Docked switches to Deferred and Done, and back. Press a section's name to show its list.

On the Mac board, the plus under the ticket list makes a new card, exactly as Command + T does. The list is only as tall as its tickets need, up to its full height, and the plus stands under its foot.

A ticket is green while an agent is working on its card, grey while your message waits for an agent to pick it up, and has a darker title while a reply is unread. A folded corner means the agent says a change is ready for you to try; a click on the corner, or Control + U, clears it.

Docked, Deferred and Done list the latest arrival first. Doing lists new cards on top, then the cards waiting on you, oldest first (Settings, Appearance, "Cards waiting on you" turns them to newest first), then the cards queued or being worked on.

Each card has four buttons in its top bar: a sun for Doing, a sun behind a cloud for Docked, a moon for Deferred and a cross for Done; the one for the card's own section is faded. The keys are Control + Shift + [ for Doing, Control + Shift + ] for Docked, Control + Shift + \ for Deferred and Control + Shift + Backspace or Delete for Done, and they work while typing. When you are not typing, [, ] and \ alone do the first three. Plain Backspace and Delete never mark a card Done. After you move the card on screen, the next card in the section it left shows. A new message from you brings a Docked or Deferred card back to Doing.

On the Mac board, in the Done section only, a garbage can shows just left of a ticket while the pointer is over it or the paper beside it. Doing, Docked and Deferred never show it. One click on the can deletes that card at once, with no menu and no question: it leaves every section and goes to the Bin on the settings page (see [The settings page](settings.md#the-settings-page)), and if it was the card on screen, the next card in its section shows. There is no key for it yet, and the phone has no can. A card in the Bin keeps its thread, attachments, queued messages and magic box, but no agent is handed it and no page or phone shows it; a claim held on it is let go with its messages still waiting, and a reply sent to it is refused. After 30 days in the Bin the board deletes it for good, checked when the board starts and once a day.

Agents link related cards to each other, such as a card that continues or depends on another, across projects too; the board keeps the links with the cards, through the Bin as well, but does not show them yet.

Agents also note on a card the git branch its work is on and a few words on where that work stands, and read them back with the card's number, project, section and when it was made and last changed, which the board already records; the notes stay with the card, through the Bin as well, and the board does not show any of it yet.

## The Mac board's top bar

Along the top of the Mac board, from the left: a Home button, which opens the home page and stays pressed while it is open; a pill with the open project's name (while the home page is up, the name of the project you came from, with a down chevron after it); and a few faint project names. At the right end, evenly spaced, the speaker mutes the reply chime, the bug opens the feedback box on its own (see "Improvements" under [The settings page](settings.md#the-settings-page)), and the squid opens the settings page.

Click the pill to open the list of every open project, in your order, each with its number (the one Command + 1 to 9 opens it by), and the ones holding unread replies in bold. Click a project to switch to it. Rest the pointer on a row and a cross shows at its right end that closes that project (the last one stays open). Hold a row and drag it up or down to reorder the projects; drag it out of the list and let go to open that project in a window of its own. "Open a new folder", the last row, opens the board's folder picker and adds the folder you choose as a project (see [The folder picker](#the-folder-picker)). Escape or a click outside closes the list. Closed projects come back from the home page. On the home page, a click on the pill's name opens the project you came from, and a click on the chevron after the name opens the list, with no project checked.

The faint names beside the pill are open projects with unread replies that were active in the last 7 days, newest first. When there are none, they are the 3 with the most unread replies, then the most recently active. The project you just left is always first, and there are never more than 4. On the home page the project you came from is the pill's name, so it is not among them. Click one to switch to it: it moves into the pill and the project you left appears first beside it.

Command + 1 to 9 opens the projects in the list's order.

## The home page

The Home button at the left of the top bar opens the home page, above every project. A board with no project open starts there. The phone has the same page, opened from Home at the top of its project list.

The left half lists your projects. Click one to open its board (a closed one opens again). "Open a new folder" opens the board's folder picker and adds the folder you choose as a project.

The right half shows "Token consumption per day": the tokens Claude Code and Codex have used on this Mac each day, as a heatmap of the last 365 days (a calendar of shaded squares) or a line chart of this year so far, January 1 to today, picked with the switch in its corner. Scroll or drag sideways for earlier weeks, and point at a day for its numbers. The counts are read from the logs Claude Code and Codex already keep on this Mac; only the counts are kept. `token_logs` in `run.config.json` can point at other log folders, or turn a tool off with an empty list.

The download button beside the switch saves the chart on show as a high resolution PNG (3:1 for the heatmap, 2:1 for the line) of the whole range with the Facilitator name on it, and on the line also marks the day this board first started; the Mac downloads it and the phone opens its share sheet.

Under it, "Usage Limits" shows how much of each plan limit is used, with when it was last updated: Claude 5-hour, Claude weekly, Codex 5-hour and Codex weekly, each only when there is a number. Codex's come from Codex itself. Claude's come through Claude Code's status line, which the installer does not set up: add `"statusLine": {"type": "command", "command": "python3 <folder>/claude-statusline.py"}` to `~/.claude/settings.json`, where `<folder>` is the folder [the runbook](../app/RUNBOOK.md) is in (`app/` in the folder you cloned). To keep a status line you already have, put its command after the script as one quoted argument. Claude Code sends limits only on Pro and Max plans, after a session's first reply.

## The folder picker

"Open a new folder", at the foot of the pill's list and on the home page and the new tab, opens a folder picker the board draws itself, the same on any system the board runs on. Your places (your home folder, Desktop, Documents and Downloads) and your machines run down the left; today the only machine is this one. The folders show as a list, with when each was last changed and how many items it holds, or as columns, one per folder, with the path along the foot. The switch beside the arrows picks between them, and the board remembers your choice. The back arrow goes up one folder and the forward arrow back down; the pop-up names the folder on show and the folders above it, up to your home folder. Type or paste a path in "Go to" and press Return to go there. New folder makes a folder inside the one on show once you name it. Choose adds the folder on show as a project; Cancel, the cross or Escape closes the picker.

With the keys: the up and down arrows move through the folders, Return or the right arrow opens the one reached, Return with none reached chooses the folder on show, and the left arrow goes up. While the picker is open the board's own keys wait.

The picker lists only your home folder and what is inside it, and leaves hidden folders out, along with links that lead out of your home folder or into a hidden one. In a very large folder, or on a slow disk, it shows the folders it could read in time and says that not every folder is listed. The first time it opens Desktop, Documents or Downloads, macOS may ask once whether the board may read it; a folder the board may not read says so in the picker.

## Browsing and selecting cards

A card on screen is either browsed or selected, on the Mac board and the phone alike. Browsing (the arrow keys, a swipe on the phone, switching projects, opening the board) shows a card without marking it read. Selecting (Enter, or a click or tap on the card or its ticket) marks it read, and so does typing in it. A reply that lands on the selected card counts as read only while the board's window is in front. The ticket of the card on screen is always lifted, so the list shows where you are. A double click on the empty board (on the phone, a double tap on the margin round the card) lowers the open card; showing any card raises it again.

An empty typing box shows a light-hearted line in grey. A card keeps its line until a message is sent on it, then takes a new one, drawn at random from a shuffled bag of the twenty so none comes back until all have been shown. Each device keeps its own lines (in the browser's localStorage), and a card that is deleted loses its entry.

What you type in a card's typing box and do not send is kept on the device as you type, with any files that have finished uploading for it, so a reload, an app restart or a force close puts it back on that card with the caret at its end. A file still uploading is not kept. A message the board has taken, or emptying the box and taking its files out, clears it; a card that is deleted loses it. On the Mac board a message the board has not taken yet, still on its way or marked as not sent, is kept as well, and after a reload it is back in the box ahead of anything typed after it (the phone keeps its unsent messages and sends them again). Drafts stay on the device they were typed on (the Mac board and its narrow phone view share one); nothing about them goes to the board.

With "Format text while typing" on (Settings, Editor), the typing box draws the Markdown you type the way Obsidian's live preview does, and sends the Markdown itself. Bold, italic, struck, code and linked words show their marks only while the caret is in them. A heading takes its size once its # and space are typed, and shows its hashes only while the caret is on its line. A quote shows its bar once its > and space are typed. A dash, star, plus or number with its space becomes a list item at once, under a line of text as much as on a line of its own. A new line (Shift + Enter, or return on the phone's own keyboard) carries a list's marker or a quote's > down; on an empty item it ends the list, or moves a nested item out a level, and on an empty quoted line it ends the quote. Backspace right after a marker takes the whole marker. Tab nests a list item under the one above it and Shift + Tab brings it back out. Words pasted from a web page keep their formatting, as Markdown. Option + up and down go to the start and the end of a paragraph, with Shift a paragraph at a time. The caret steps over a quote's > in one move, since it is not shown.

On the Mac board a long reply grows the typing box in two steps. It grows a line at a time to its usual height; once what you type runs past that, it stands at once at its tallest, which leaves the first two lines of the agent's response in sight under the card's title and your messages above them, whatever the card's size. At that step the response is piped out beside the card, to read while you type: it stands where the magic box would, on the right, or on the left over the ticket list and the clock, which fade well down while it is there, when the card's magic box stands on the right or the right has no room. It is placed, sized and scrolled as the magic box is and rises and sinks as it does, with no edge and no bar, its words fading out at both ends, and a dashed bracket, } on the right and { on the left, joining it to the card. Sending the reply, or cutting it back under the first step, puts the response back into the card. The phone and the Mac's narrow phone view keep one step.

The keys, on the Mac board and on the phone with a paired keyboard:

- Left and right arrows: the previous or next card. Control + Shift + left and right do the same while typing.
- Enter: select the card and start typing. In the typing box, Enter sends, Shift + Enter starts a new line, and Command + Enter sends without moving.
- Enter twice, or Control + Shift + Enter: send, then go to the card that has waited longest on you (with nothing typed, Control + Shift + Enter only moves). Control + Enter alone is not bound: on an iPhone with a keyboard the system opens its copy menu on it. On the phone, Enter twice sends and then opens the card list.
- Escape: leave the typing box, then let go of the card; it also closes a menu or a picture.
- Control + R: a random card from the list that is neither being worked on nor waiting for an agent.
- Control + Shift + up and down: the card's older and newer replies.
- Control + S: hold to scroll the answer down, faster the longer you hold; tap and then hold to scroll up. With your keys in the card's magic box (put there by Control + Option + right, or by a click in it) it scrolls the box instead, its words or a page an agent made, the same way; with your keys in the response piped out beside a long reply, it scrolls that; with your keys on the card it is the card that scrolls.
- Control + Shift + W and Control + Shift + S: open or close the card's top and bottom message bubbles, the grey ones holding your own messages (on top, the ones the reply answered; at the bottom, the ones still waiting, the lowest bubble when there are several), as a click on the bubble's arrow does. A bubble short enough to show whole has no arrow, and a card without that bubble ignores the key.
- Control + U: clear a ticket's ready-to-test corner.
- Control + Shift + [ , ] , \ and Backspace or Delete: move the card to Doing, Docked, Deferred or Done.
- Control + Shift + ' and ;: the next or previous section of the ticket list.
- Command + T: a new card. Command + 1 to 9: switch project, in the order of the pill's list. Command + 0 (the Mac board and the installed phone app; a Safari tab does not take it, because Safari on iPad uses it to reset zoom): the home page, as the Home button does; it works while typing and does nothing on the home page.
- Tab: steps from the card's title to its typing box to the card itself, and round; Shift + Tab goes the other way. On a card that already has a title, the first Tab goes to the typing box, and so does the first Tab after you come back to the card or click it. In the typing box with "Format text while typing" on, on a list item, Tab and Shift + Tab nest the item and bring it back out instead.
- Control + Option + up and down: move between the card's title, its answer and its typing box, top to bottom. Up goes from the typing box to the answer to the title, down comes back, and they stop at either end; with nothing focused, up goes to the answer and down to the typing box. In the answer a caret stands that types nothing: Shift with the arrows selects, with Option a word at a time and with Command to the end of a line or of the answer; Command + A selects the whole answer and nothing else; Command + C copies. Escape or a click elsewhere leaves it. While the response is piped out beside a long reply, it is the answer: up from the typing box puts the caret in the piped response, the same way. While Apple's screen reader is on, it takes these keys.
- Control + Option + right and left: right moves your keys into the card's magic box, as a click in it would, when the card on screen has one open, whether you are on the bare card or typing in its typing box; the box becomes the raised one and the card settles, and the box's own keys work there (Control + S scrolls its words or its page, Command + A selects its words). Left brings you back to exactly where you were on the card: the typing box with its caret and selection where they were, or the card itself if you were not typing. With no box open on the card, or left when your keys are not in the box, nothing happens and the key is left alone. If you have clicked or tabbed into a page an agent made, left still brings you back; a website in the box keeps every key typed in it to itself, so there left cannot be heard and a click on the card brings you back. With the response piped out beside a long reply, the key toward it puts your keys in it, with the answer's caret at its first line on show, and the key away from it brings you back to where you were; toward the magic box the keys are the box's as before. While Apple's screen reader is on, it takes these keys.
- On the phone, Control + Shift + comma opens and shuts the card list; with it out, up and down walk the tickets, Enter selects the lifted one and shuts the list, and Escape just shuts it.
- On the phone, Control + Shift + period opens and shuts the card's magic box the same way. Either key swaps out whatever else is open: the card list, the box or the settings. Between the list and the box the card stays down, and one takes the other's place. On a card with no magic box the period opens nothing, and the card bounces to show the key was heard.

The keys with Command or Control also work from a card's typing box and title. The board turns off Chrome shortcuts that would get in the way, such as Save Page, Print, zoom and new tabs; Command + F still finds and Command + R still reloads.

A link in a reply opens a small menu with Copy link and Open in browser (an ordinary Chrome window on the Mac, the phone's browser on the phone); an attachment's link adds Download. A click or tap on a picture shows it large: on the phone as large as the screen holds, on the Mac in the Settings window's place. Pinch it, on the phone's screen or the Mac's trackpad, to zoom up to four times; zoomed, drag it or scroll with two fingers to look around. A double tap or double click zooms to twice at that point and back. At its fitted size a click or tap puts it back; zoomed, Escape does, which works at any size. On the phone, hold a finger on a picture for its menu.

## Seeing a card's magic box

An agent can open a magic box beside a card to show you a picture, a chart, a page it made or a website. It shows only while you are on that card, and it never takes your keys. On the Mac board, Control + Option + right puts your keys in it, as a click would, and Control + Option + left puts them back on the card, in your typing box where you were typing. On the Mac board it stands beside the card and never over it: in a window too narrow to give it room there, it is not shown, and it comes back by itself as soon as the window is wide enough again. On the phone, on a card that has one, a pull from the right edge, right to left, brings it out above the card; Control + Shift + period brings it too. On the Mac board, whichever of the card and the box you last clicked into is shown in full and the other is faded; a card with no box never fades. On the phone the box is for reading only. The box takes the size of what it shows, and long text scrolls inside it. On the Mac board that holds for every kind, up to the box's largest size: a picture in its own shape, text at its own height, and a page an agent made at the height of its own contents (a website, which the board cannot measure, keeps the full size). Also on the Mac board, two dashed lines join the card to its box, one about a tenth of the box's height down from its top and one the same share up from its bottom, so they keep the same look on a box of any size. The box has a bar with a cross at its right, which closes the box until an agent fills it again. On the Mac board, in a portrait window too, the bar is at the box's top; on the phone it is at the box's bottom, so the cross is in the box's bottom right corner. A website that will not be shown inside the box appears as a square with its name, and a click on it opens the site in a new page, on the Mac in your own Chrome.

## The reply chime

The Mac board plays a soft two-note bell when an agent's reply makes a card your turn; a burst of replies rings once. While the board's window is in front, replies on the project you are looking at stay quiet. Chrome lets a page play sound only after a click or key press in it, so the chime works from your first one. The speaker at the right end of the top bar mutes it. The phone gets notifications instead (see [The phone page and the bridge](phone.md)).
