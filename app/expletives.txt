# The curse words the board saves as [expletive] when Settings, Editor,
# "Replace curse words" is on. expletives.py reads this file and says how
# each kind of entry is matched; the file is read again when it changes.
#
# One entry a line, under one of three headings. An entry is one word of the
# letters a to z; case does not matter, and # starts a comment.
#
#   [anywhere]  a root matched anywhere, even inside a longer word, for roots
#               no normal English word holds ("unfuckingbelievable" becomes
#               "un[expletive]believable")
#   [alone]     a word matched only on its own, for roots that sit inside
#               normal words ("you ass" becomes "you [expletive]", and
#               "a classy assessment" is left alone)
#   [never]     a normal word left as it is although it holds a root above
#
# Every letter of a root may repeat ("fuuuck"), and every root takes the
# endings -s, -es, -ies, -ed, -d, -er, -ers, -ery, -ing, -ings, -in, -in',
# -y, -ier and -iest, so "fucking" and "asses" need no line of their own.
#
# Sources. The swear words and slurs are taken from the English list of "List
# of Dirty, Naughty, Obscene, and Otherwise Bad Words" by Shutterstock and its
# contributors, https://github.com/LDNOOBW/List-of-Dirty-Naughty-Obscene-and-Otherwise-Bad-Words
# (file "en", as of its 2020-07-13 commit), licensed under the Creative Commons
# Attribution 4.0 International licence, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
# Changes: only the swear words and slurs are kept (its sex-act, anatomy,
# pornography and drug words, and words with a common normal meaning, are
# left out), multi-word entries are cut to the root they hold, and each word
# is sorted into the headings below. Added to it: the curse words and spellings
# a scan of real messages found ("python3 app/expletives.py scan"), common
# English swear words the list does not have, and the normal words under
# [never], found by running the list over the system dictionary.
